Observe and learn

For u = 30 m/s, imagine the v–t graph from t = 0 to t = 2 s. What would the area under it represent?

Click ▶ Play to see the graph and the area take shape, and the result emerge.

The v–t graph is a straight line, so the area under it is a trapezium. Its parallel sides are the velocities u = 30 m/s and v = 10 m/s, and its width is Δt = 2 s.

Area = ½ (u + v) Δt = ½ (30 + 10) × 2 = 40 m

That is the displacement formula from page C. So the area under a v–t graph between two instants equals the displacement during that interval.

What is the displacement between t = 2 s and t = 4 s?

Zero. v goes from +10 m/s to −10 m/s, so the mean velocity is 0. On the graph, the area above the time axis (2 → 3 s, +5 m) cancels the area below it (3 → 4 s, −5 m): the ball rises 5 m and falls back 5 m.
